1 · The setting

Gas pipes, machines, people, and a few detectors.

A factory runs gas over head-height racks and down to every machine. When a fitting fails, hydrogen rises, propane sinks, the ventilation carries it, and within a minute a cloud sits between people and the doors.

4 · How: three steps, each a TabPFN model

From a few noisy detectors to a route for every person, every 15 seconds.

1

Find the leak

Rayan's locator reads every detector, masts included, and returns where the leak is, how strong, and how high.

Usual: triangulate on the strongest detector.

2

Predict the gas

Ahmad's hazard model predicts, for every spot in the hall, the chance it turns toxic or flammable in the next minute.

Usual: CFD, minutes per run, too slow to react.

3

Route everyone

Steven's planner gives each person a way out around the predicted gas and balances the doors so nobody queues in the cloud.

Usual: shortest path to the nearest sign.

5 · Scaling to three dimensions

Light gas rises, heavy gas sinks. So the detectors climb the pipes.

Every vertical pipe gets a mast of three detectors. The gas is simulated in 3D, calibrated on real plume measurements, and people breathe what is between 0.5 and 2 m.
