System overview

Three models, one loop, every 15 s.

Hover a block.

Step 1 · Source localisation (2D)

Where is the leak, and how strong?

Input · sensor history: the readings of every gas sensor over the last 30 s, and the wind.

Output · probability of each floor cell being the source; release-rate quantiles.

TabPFN · classifies the source zone in context from 1,200 simulated leaks of the same plant. No gradient training.

Baselines · prior (uniform over the pipes), concentration-weighted centroid of the sensors, k-nearest neighbours on the same rows.

Step 2 · Hazard forecast (2D)

Where will the air be harmful in the next minute?

Input · per cell: down- and cross-wind distance to the estimated source, time, wind, release rate, mixing.

Output · P(toxic) and P(flammable) per cell, 0–120 s ahead.

TabPFN · regresses the concentration distribution in context; probabilities come from its predictive quantiles.

Baseline · deterministic physics · the same advection–diffusion model that generates the plumes, run once from the estimated source at nominal mixing. One answer, no uncertainty.

TabPFN matches it where its assumptions hold and beats it when the mixing differs (left and right of the chart). Real gas never matches a model's assumptions exactly, which is where a learned, probabilistic forecast pays off.

Step 3 · Crowd routing (2D)

A personal route for everyone, around the forecast.

Algorithm · CCRP: shortest paths on the floor graph with exit capacities, so people spread over doors instead of queueing in the cloud.

Use of TabPFN · not inside the search. Each step into a cell costs length + w · P(harm), with P(harm) from step 2. Replanned at every update.

Scaling to 3D

The same three steps in a 9 m high hall.

  • Geometry · 1 m voxels; machines, pipe racks (4.2 m) and vertical pipes with real heights.
  • Gas · time-stepped 3D dispersion around walls and machines; light gas rises, heavy gas sinks near the leak; people breathe 0.5–2 m.
  • Sensors · on walls at the gas's height, plus masts of three sensors on every vertical pipe.
  • Models · the source model also returns the leak height; the hazard model predicts per voxel.
  • Test · 9 plants, 4 never seen; same leaks for every method.

Generalisation to real measurements

Few public gas-leak datasets exist. TabPFN still transfers.

Four public datasets, each used only as test data:

  • 1:50 industrial site · wind-tunnel fog plume, Red:Vapor.
  • Sensor grid · 6 × 5 grid with measured airflow, SRGDM.
  • Office · 27 sensors at 3 heights with HVAC, Örebro.
  • MOX tunnel · metal-oxide sensors along and across a wind tunnel, UCI.

Future work and development

From a simulated hall to real plants, robots and drones.
